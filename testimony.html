God's Faithfulness Through Every Season

The story of a faithful God who never stopped pursuing me.

Read My Testimony

If you had met me years ago, you probably would have seen someone who looked somewhat successful on the outside. I had good jobs, I could solve problems, and people came to me for answers. But like many people, there were battles inside that very few knew about. I've learned that sometimes the deepest wounds aren't the ones people can see. They are the ones we carry quietly.

When I was fifteen years old, my older sister died. The next few years brought even more loss. At sixteen, I lost my dad's mother. We were very close, and just weeks before she passed away, she told me she was glad I had decided to stay home from boarding school because now she had something to live for. A short time later, she was gone. At seventeen, I lost my mom's mother. At eighteen, I lost my mom's father.

Looking back now, I realize those weren't just isolated losses. They were the years that quietly shaped how I saw life, relationships, and even myself. I didn't realize it then, but my heart was learning to expect goodbye.

Years later, I finally married at thirty-seven years old, believing I would spend the rest of my life with my wife. Less than five years later, we were divorced. Divorce was one of the hardest seasons of my life. One of the reasons it was so difficult is because, when you have children together, the story doesn't simply end. You continue parenting, making decisions, and sharing birthdays, graduations, and family milestones. There is no clean ending. For me, it felt like grieving a relationship while still living with the reminders of what had been lost.

As an adult, God also brought men into my life who became brothers and mentors. They discipled me, challenged me, and walked with me. Then, one by one, I stood at their funerals. Larry in 2008. Steve in 2024. What still amazes me is that I had talked with both of them only hours before the Lord called them home.

Looking back, I can now see that those repeated losses left scars I didn't fully understand. I often found myself searching for affirmation, acceptance, and companionship in places that could never truly satisfy my heart.

As I've continued walking with Christ, God has been showing me something even deeper. I didn't just carry my failures—I quietly began introducing myself by them. When I looked in the mirror, I often saw the man who had failed in his marriage, the man who had made financial mistakes, and the man who had sought affirmation in the wrong places. Then I unknowingly assumed everyone else saw me the same way.

Maybe you've done that too.

Maybe when you look in the mirror, you don't first see someone redeemed by Christ. Maybe you see the addict, the divorced woman, the angry father, the person who wasn't chosen, the one who failed, or the one who never feels like they're enough.

God has been gently showing me that I wasn't seeing myself through the eyes of Christ. I was seeing myself through the lens of my wounds. He is healing that place.

My failures are chapters in my story. They are not my identity.

My identity is not “the divorced man.” My identity is not “the man who failed.” My identity is not found in my successes or my failures. My identity is that I am a beloved son of my heavenly Father, redeemed by Jesus Christ and still being sanctified by His grace.

That doesn't erase my past. It redeems it.

And it changes the way I see myself, the way I love others, and even the stories I tell myself about what others must think of me.

Yet through every season, God never stopped pursuing me.

One of the greatest blessings God has given me is my two children, Sarah Ashley and Grant. Being their father has become one of the greatest joys of my life. They have taught me more about the Father's heart than they will probably ever know.

Professionally, God has blessed me with a long career in technology. He gave me a mind that enjoys solving problems and building systems. But over the years I've learned that solving technical problems is much easier than allowing God to transform my own heart.

A few years ago, God also transformed my physical health. I lost close to eighty pounds, not because I wanted to become skinny, but because I wanted to become healthy enough to fully live the life He had given me. I wanted more years with my children. I wanted more energy to serve Him. That journey eventually led me to become a health coach, where I now have the privilege of helping others experience transformation in their own lives.

But perhaps the greatest transformation God has been doing isn't physical. It's spiritual.

Lately, God has been teaching me what it truly means to abide in Christ. For years, I knew that truth in my head. Now He is teaching it to my heart.

He has been showing me that I sometimes looked to people to provide what only He can provide. I wanted affirmation. I wanted acceptance. I wanted someone to tell me I was enough.

But God has been patiently teaching me that only the Father's love can satisfy the deepest needs of my soul.

One sentence has become deeply personal to me:

When my heart is rooted in the Father's love, affirmation from others becomes fruit, not food.

People are wonderful gifts from God, but they were never created to become my source.

Jesus is my source.

That doesn't mean life suddenly becomes easy.

I still carry scars. I still have prayers that haven't been answered. I still wake up some mornings in an empty house. I still hope that one day God may surprise me with a godly wife—not because I need someone to complete me, but because I would love someone to walk beside me as we follow Christ together, serve Him together, go on mission together, and create everyday memories together.

But whether that day comes tomorrow or years from now, I know this:

Jesus is enough.

He is teaching me to receive from Him what I once asked other people to provide.

One of the prayers I pray most often is simple:

“Father, teach me to receive from You what I have been asking others to provide.”

That prayer is changing me.

I don't stand before you as someone who has arrived. I stand before you as someone who is still being sanctified—still learning, still growing, still repenting, still trusting, and still following Jesus.

If there is one thing I have learned, it is this: God is far more interested in transforming my character than simply changing my circumstances. Sometimes He calms the storm. Sometimes He calms His child. He has done both in my life.

Maybe you're in a storm right now.

Maybe you're carrying a wound no one else can see.

Maybe you've quietly introduced yourself by your failures for so long that you've forgotten who God says you are.

If that's you, I want to encourage you.

The same Jesus who has patiently pursued me is patiently pursuing you.

The same Father who is teaching me to abide in His love is inviting you to do the same.

I don't know everything God has planned for my future. I don't know what surprises are still ahead. But I have learned enough about the heart of my Father to trust Him with the chapters I haven't read yet.

As I've learned to trust Him, I believe He's been whispering something to my heart:

“Tom(my)... live in the story I’m writing, not the one your fears are writing.”

So I continue to pray. I continue to serve. I continue to disciple others. I continue to love my children. I continue to trust.

Because my story is not over.

I still believe in what I like to call “God Surprises.”

Not because I believe life will suddenly become easy, but because I know my Father delights in writing stories that only He can receive glory for.

As I share my story, my hope isn't that you focus on me. My hope is that you see the faithfulness of God. If all you remember is what I've been through, then you've missed the point. But if you walk away believing that Jesus is faithful in the middle of your own story, then I've accomplished exactly what I hoped.

My story isn't remarkable because of what I've endured.

It's remarkable because of the faithfulness of the God who has never stopped walking with me through it.

If my life points people anywhere, I don't want it to point to my strength. I want it to point to the faithfulness of Jesus Christ.

He has never left me.

He has never abandoned me.

He has never stopped pursuing me.

And He will not stop writing my story until the day I finally see Him face to face.

Until then, I will keep abiding. I will keep trusting. I will keep serving. I will keep looking for God Surprises.

And I will keep praying,

“Your will, not mine.”

Tom Hyatt
