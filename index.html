My Story

The Soundtrack of My Journey

I’ve always felt my life was a musical.

Every chapter has a story. Every story has a song.

That’s probably because, in many ways, it was.

My parents filled our home with music. They both sang in our church choir, the city concert choir, and local musical theater productions. My mother even performed at the historic Barter Theatre in Abingdon, Virginia. Music wasn’t something we did occasionally—it was simply part of who we were.

As a child, I loved musicals. I didn’t just watch them; I stepped into them. Songs from Brigadoon, West Side Story, Oliver!, They’re Playing Our Song, and many others became part of my imagination. I found myself blending their stories into my own, letting the music give voice to feelings I didn’t yet know how to express.

Then Christian music entered my life.

I can still remember singing Here I Am, Lord by Dan Schutte as a young boy. I didn’t fully understand everything those words meant, but something deep inside me knew they mattered. Looking back now, I believe God was already planting seeds that would shape the rest of my life.

I followed my love for music through high school and college, singing wherever I could. During my senior year of high school, I had the privilege of being selected to the All-State Choir. Singing wasn’t just something I enjoyed—it was one of the places where I felt most alive.

Yet there was always one dream that remained just beyond my reach.

More than anything, I wanted to play the piano or the guitar. I could hear songs in my heart, but I didn’t have the ability to bring them to life. The lyrics would come. The emotions were real. The stories were there. But they remained locked away because I couldn’t create the music that surrounded them.

Life, however, kept writing new chapters.

I became a father. I built a career as an engineer. I experienced success and failure, love and loss, seasons of confidence and seasons of doubt. Friends I dearly loved passed away. My children grew up far faster than I ever imagined. God opened doors for me to serve on mission trips around the world, where I witnessed His faithfulness in ways I’ll never forget.

Through every one of those moments, something inside me kept doing what it had always done.

It searched for the song.

Sometimes it became a prayer.

Sometimes it became worship.

Sometimes it wrestled with questions.

Sometimes it simply helped me remember.

But there was always a song.

Looking back, I realize this is how God made me. When life happens—good or bad—I instinctively try to capture it in a song.

For years, those songs lived only in notebooks and scattered pages because I had no way to give them a musical voice.

Today, I finally do.

Every song I release begins with a real moment. Sometimes it’s about God’s grace. Sometimes it’s about the love between a father and his children. Sometimes it’s about losing someone you love. Sometimes it’s about the hope of Christ’s return. Sometimes it’s about a mission trip to Africa. Sometimes it’s about a memory that refuses to fade.

They’re not random ideas.

They’re chapters.

I don’t sit down and ask myself, “What kind of song should I write next?”

I simply live the next chapter of my life.

Then I ask, “What’s the song this chapter is trying to sing?”

Sometimes the answer comes in minutes. Sometimes it takes years.

That’s why my music isn’t centered around one style or one topic. Some songs are deeply rooted in worship. Others tell stories of family, friendship, laughter, heartbreak, or redemption. They’re different because life is different.

I’m not inviting people to simply listen to my music.

I’m inviting them to walk the journey with me.

If something I’ve experienced helps someone feel less alone, points them toward Christ, or reminds them that God is still writing their story too, then the song has done exactly what I hoped it would do.

This isn’t just a catalog of music.

It’s the soundtrack of a life still being written.

One story from the Barter Theatre has stayed with me my entire life.

Robert Porterfield, the theater’s founder, was known for ending performances by saying:

“If you like us, talk about us. If you don’t, keep your mouth shut.”

I’ve laughed at that line for years, but I’ve also lived by it.

I’ve never wanted to pressure people into liking what I create. Whether it’s my work, my coaching, my leadership, my writing, or now my music, I’d rather let the work speak for itself. If it encourages someone, moves them, or points them closer to Christ, I hope they’ll tell someone else.

That’s always felt more genuine than trying to convince people to listen.

So, if one of my songs encourages you...

Tell someone.

And, well... in the spirit of Robert Porterfield...

If you don’t like the music... keep your mouth shut. 😉

My Music

The Stories Behind the Songs

I’m Still Here

Some songs begin with a melody.

Some begin with a lyric.

This one didn’t.

It began with a man who didn’t know what else to do.

I’ve wrestled with these feelings more than once throughout my life. There have been seasons of loneliness. Seasons of discouragement. Seasons where I wondered what God was doing and why everything seemed so quiet.

But those feelings reached a boiling point during the last time I was laid off from work.

For nearly six months, I searched for another job. Every application brought hope. Every rejection brought another wave of disappointment. The days became long. The silence became loud. It wasn’t just the uncertainty of unemployment—it was the weight of wondering where I fit, whether I still had purpose, and why it felt like life was moving forward for everyone else while I stood still.

During that season, I felt a lot like Job.

It wasn’t that I believed God had abandoned me—I never stopped believing He was there. But it seemed like everything around me had been stripped away. I was lonely. I was exhausted. I was carrying questions I couldn’t answer, praying prayers that often seemed to be met with silence.

I wasn’t trying to write a song.

I was simply trying to survive.

There were days when just making it to bedtime felt like a victory. So I started writing—not lyrics, not poetry, just honest thoughts. I needed somewhere to put the pain that kept circling through my mind. I wasn’t trying to sound creative. I wasn’t thinking about verses or choruses. I was simply having conversations with God on paper.

It was journaling more than songwriting.

Page after page became prayers that no one else would ever read. They were honest. Sometimes messy. Sometimes angry. Sometimes full of questions. Sometimes there were no answers—only silence.

One detail about this song has always stayed with me.

The title didn’t come after I wrote the lyrics.

It came long before there was ever a song.

During those months of unemployment, loneliness, and uncertainty, I found myself praying the same words over and over again. Some days they weren’t even calm prayers. They were cries.

“God... I’m still here.”

Not as a statement of confidence.

As a plea.

“God... I’m still here.”

“I’m still waiting.”

“I’m still hurting.”

“I’m still looking for You.”

That phrase became part of my conversations with God long before it ever became a chorus.

As I looked back over those journal pages, I noticed something unexpected.

Some of those prayers already had rhythm.

Some of those thoughts already sounded like lyrics.

The emotions were so real that they naturally began to take the shape of songs.

I’m Still Here is one of the very first.

It’s not a song about having all the answers. It’s not even a song about finding immediate victory. It’s the prayer of someone who is exhausted but hasn’t completely let go.

From the chorus

God, I need You to do something now.
I’m running out of strength, I don’t know how.
I’m not asking for easy, I’m asking for true.
If I’m still breathing, it’s got to be You.

Those aren’t words I invented because they sounded good.

Those are words I lived.

The bridge may be the most vulnerable part of the song:

From the bridge

If You don’t come and do something soon,
I don’t know how much longer I can stand.
I know You’re there, I know You’re true.
I just can’t walk this road alone.

That wasn’t written to be dramatic.

It was an honest prayer from someone who still believed God was there but desperately needed Him to move.

Looking back now, I realize something I couldn’t see then.

God wasn’t only listening to those prayers.

He was quietly teaching me to write.

The journal pages that carried me through one of the hardest seasons of my life became the beginning of a journey I never expected—a journey of writing songs that tell the truth about pain, faith, doubt, hope, and the God who meets us in the middle of all of it.

Eventually, God answered those prayers. He opened a door to a new job after months of waiting. But even more than that, He gave me something I never saw coming.

He gave me a voice.

I didn’t set out to become a songwriter—and I really don’t think I am.

I set out to survive.

Somewhere along the way, my journal became lyrics, my prayers became songs, and my pain became part of my testimony.

That’s where I’m Still Here was born.

And if this song reminds someone else that they can bring their deepest hurt honestly before God—that they can keep talking to Him even when heaven seems silent—then every one of those difficult journal pages was worth writing.
